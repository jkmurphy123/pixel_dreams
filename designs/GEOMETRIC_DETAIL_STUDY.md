## The six techniques

| # | technique | level 4 edge count* | read |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 | **Band stack** | 24 | Your suggestion exactly: N horizontal slices, one seam ring per cut. Cheapest; at level 4 it still reads as a sliced box, not a solid one. |
| 2 | **Face grid** | 48 | Every face divided N × N. The natural "more solid wireframe" option; back-face grids show through, which adds density but can look busy. |
| 3 | **Volume lattice** | 87 lines | Full N×N×N internal grid. Very dense and solid-looking; the interior clutter is strong and the line count grows fastest. |
| 4 | **Floor & mullion** | 42 | Floor rings plus vertical wall ribs, with a roof grid. Architectural and deliberately solid; a direct upgrade of the band-stack idea. |
| 5 | **Truss grid** | 144 | Face grid plus alternating diagonals. Industrial/structural; the busiest option. |
| 6 | **Veil panels** | 30 + fills | Visible faces filled dark, then gridded. The most solid read; however it abandons the transparent neon look and needs face visibility/depth sorting for every object. |

\* Counts are drawn lines for one cube, not every cell edge; they are the useful
relative cost.  Level 1 is the same basic 12-edge cube for every technique.

## Recommendation

**Floor & Mullion is the best balance**: it is a direct evolution of your
band-stack idea, it makes a box read as a lit structure rather than an empty
cage, and it costs about the same as a full face grid.  **Face Grid** is the
fallback if you prefer an even, all-over mesh.  **Veil Panels** is the most
solid but is a different art direction and a much bigger renderer change.

Band stack alone is probably too subtle at level 4; if you want that exact
look, use levels 2–6 and pair it with the vertical ribs (which is technique 4).

## Open questions for the chosen technique

- Detail range: 1–4, 1–5, or higher?  Each level is N divisions along an axis.
- Apply the same rule to pyramids, prisms, rings and constructs?  Floor &
  Mullion and Band Stack map naturally to boxes; a face grid maps to any
  polygon face; a filled veil needs face normals.
- Edge-budget cost: the detail should be bounded by the existing quality
  controller, especially at level 4 on 1080p.


## Decision

Volume lattice, levels 1–4, was chosen.  The implementation adds a lazy volume
lattice to near box towers, matching ring detail to non-box proc shapes, and
loads landmarks one level above the selected setting (`min(5, detail + 1)`).
The detailed geometry is also what the bottom-right landmark inset shows.
